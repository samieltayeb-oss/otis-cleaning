PART ONE — THE JOB
1.1 · Two converted clients, as fast as possible
That is the whole job right now. Not a pipeline, not a brand, not a marketing programme — two businesses that were not Otis clients before, and are Otis clients after.


Everything after Part One is the plan for what happens next. It exists because two clients won by luck repeat nothing, while two clients won by a method can be repeated next month and the month after — which is the difference between a good week and a business that grows.
What counts as converted
* A job booked, delivered and invoiced. Not a quote issued, not a promising conversation, not a walkthrough that went well.
* A place on a backup list is a foothold, not a client — until the first call-out lands and is paid. It is tracked separately for exactly that reason.
Why two, and not twenty
Capacity is 3–5 medium recurring contracts, or 2 large daily facilities, plus unlimited one-off work. That is a hard constraint, not a target.


A pipeline built beyond it produces a delivery failure, and a delivery failure costs reviews — which are the constraint on referrals, on being found, and on every close that follows. The tracker shows capacity remaining on the Pipeline tab so it cannot be forgotten in a good week.

PART TWO — HOW WE DO IT
2.1 · The way in
Nobody wins a cleaning contract cold
Most businesses Otis wants already have a cleaner — this is the consistent account from commercial cleaning operators describing their own pipelines, not an assumption. Winning a contract cold therefore means displacing their current cleaner, which needs a trigger nobody can manufacture: a service failure, a contract expiry, a budget review, a new facilities manager.


Cold approaches that ask for that decision fail, and they fail slowly — which is worse, because they consume the weeks that a one-off job would have converted in.


So the contract is approached sideways, through two doors that need no displacement at all.
Route one — the one-off job
Floor stripping and waxing, post-construction cleanup, a deep clean, a move-out. This is additive work the current cleaner does not do, so nobody has to be sacked for Otis to be hired. That single fact is why one-off work converts on cold outreach and contracts do not.


It is also a paid trial in both directions. The buyer risks nothing — no commitment, no notice period, no awkward conversation with an existing supplier. Otis gets inside the building, and the facilities manager watches the checklist standard happen in front of them instead of reading a claim about it on a website.
Route two — the backup list
"Put us on your list for when your cleaner doesn't turn up."


No budget, no decision, no displacement, no risk. It asks a facilities manager for nothing except permission to be remembered — the easiest yes in the industry. And when the current cleaner no-shows, which in this trade happens, Otis is the call that gets made, arrives, and performs. The comparison gets made for the buyer instead of argued at them.
Where recurring work actually fits
Recurring contracts are a retention and expansion play, not an acquisition play. The only cold exceptions are the three cases where no displacement is needed: a building with no cleaner at all, a contract with a live expiry date visible on SEAO, or genuine expressed dissatisfaction.


A one-off job is never presented as the end of the story — not to the prospect, not in the tracker, not in the copy. See 4.1.
One decision, and one open question
Not upselling the three existing sites — a decision. It is the fastest money on paper and the riskiest move available: raising the commercial temperature on a relationship that is not yet anchored by a strong profile invites that client to go and see what else is out there, in a crowded market, at nine reviews. Those three are the referral source, the review source and the proof. Referrals from them are very much in; selling to them is not.


Residential — open, and not yet discussed. Nothing about it has been decided. See 4.3.
2.2 · What we sell
1. Floor stripping, waxing and sealing — the lead offer. Montreal winters mean salt and grit for months, and it destroys hard floors. The window to strip, wax and seal is before that season, not after. This gives a cold approach the one thing cold approaches almost never have: a real reason to act now that isn't manufactured. Otis does floor care in-house where many competitors subcontract it, which makes it simultaneously the strongest genuine differentiator available.


2. Post-construction and post-renovation. Deadline-driven and urgent — a site must be clean for handover, so the urgency is real and the buyer is a contractor rather than a facilities manager. Sourced from commercial fit-outs and general contractors. ⚠️ Open: whether post-reno work inside an édifice public is décret-covered. Worth roughly $7/hr on the cost basis. One call: CPEEP, 514 384-6640. Settle it before quoting any GC.


3. Deep clean and high-dusting. Post-summer and back-to-school timing. The natural fit for schools, kindergartens and daycares.


4. Commercial move-in / move-out. New tenancy trigger. Commercial only.


And the backup-list ask on every call, whatever the outcome. A no to the job is still a yes to the list, and the list is a contract pipeline that costs nothing to hold.
Two pricing rules
No deposit. Researched and dropped as policy. Deposits are not a norm in commercial B2B cleaning — net-30 is — and asking for one marks Otis as unusual at exactly the moment it needs to look standard. The single exception worth keeping is general-contractor and post-construction work, where payment risk is real and a deposit is defensible.


Never quote a price before a walkthrough. The market runs roughly 15–30 cents per square foot. Otis's published floor of 6 cents sits below market and should never go out cold — it anchors the buyer low and devalues the work before anyone has seen the building.
How the outreach is written so it cannot rot
Only the first touch is written in full. Everything after it is written as a branch keyed to what happened, not as a dated draft:

A branch cannot go stale because it has not been triggered yet. Every sequence also carries a written on and a valid until date, and anything the tracker marks stale is re-sourced and rewritten rather than re-sent.
What the copy opens on
The prospect's own position, never Otis:


* Floor care: salt season starts in weeks, and stripping after it starts costs more than sealing before it does
* Daycares, kindergartens and CPEs: equipment must be disinfected outside children's presence — which means evenings, which is when Otis works
* Anyone using a cheap contractor: the décret's joint-liability rule (see 2.5)


Offer a walkthrough, not a pitch. A quote to keep on file costs the prospect nothing and presumes no need. The proven low-pressure opener: "we have a manager in your area on [day] — can we stop by and give you a quote to keep on file for next time you're shopping around?" It asks for no decision.


Positioning — not "quality, reliability, versatility." Every competitor's homepage says that, so none of them differentiates. The defensible ground:


1. A written checklist per site with spot-checks — verifiable, not a promise
2. Training aimed at preventing complaints rather than reacting to them
3. Floor care done in-house, not subcontracted
4. Local to the anglophone corridor — NDG-based, not a downtown call centre


English only for now. French comes later, for customer-facing material.

Schools, kindergartens and daycares are three separate sectors, never merged. A private school buys through a business manager, a preschool co-op through a volunteer parent board, a CPE under subsidised rules. They look alike on a map and behave nothing alike as buyers.


Offices have two buyers, depending on the lease. Under a gross lease the landlord cleans; under modified gross or triple-net the tenant buys its own in-suite cleaning; common areas are always the landlord's. One question settles it on the call: "do you arrange your own cleaning, or does the building do it?" In this corridor the building route is the stronger of the two — one relationship covers the lobby and entrance matting that take the worst salt damage, plus every suite in it.


Public-board schools are a tender, not a cold call. English Montreal School Board and Lester B. Pearson School Board are public bodies; their cleaning goes through SEAO on the contract-expiry trigger. Private and independent schools buy directly, and several run more than one campus.


The chasers. A script bound to the sheet, reading by header name rather than column position, so inserting a column never breaks it. Daily it flags expiring triggers, A-leads sitting untouched and rows missing evidence. Weekly it emails a digest. Nothing rots quietly.


The SEAO watch. Québec's public tender data — the only place you can see who currently cleans a public body, for how much, and when that contract was awarded, which converts directly into a renewal window with a date attached. The SEAO website itself is JavaScript-only, so the watch runs against the open data feed with a three-gate filter and a deduplication log.


Referrals. One ask, made properly, to each of the three existing site managers: who else do you know running a building that needs this? Daycare and school administrators in Montreal know each other through CPE and board networks, so the reach is far wider than three conversations. Made safe by asking only for a referral and never selling them anything.


Local groups. West Island Facebook groups, watched for "can anyone recommend a commercial cleaner" posts. That is inbound, not cold, and it is the highest-priority lead type in the system. Known groups: West Island Community Montreal · West Island Community · Montreal Business Networking · Montreal Small Business Owners · SHOP LOCAL West Island & Montreal · West Island/Dorval/Lachine Sell-Buy-Advertise. That list came from a single search and a complete sweep is still outstanding.


LinkedIn and Instagram. LinkedIn by job title, not company — Facilities Manager, Property Manager, Office Manager, Building Manager — the only channel that reaches the decision-maker as a role. Instagram for general contractors, commercial interior designers, architects and commercial realtors, who post finished fit-outs pre-occupancy: the post-construction trigger, plus the building and the contractor in one.


Event management partnerships. Repeat, scheduled demand with no cleaner to displace, and one agency relationship yields many jobs. It behaves like a property manager relationship rather than a lead, and it is the one sector with nothing sourced yet.


Division of labour. The partners work the phone — B2B cold calls in Quebec are exempt from the National DNCL. Faris works email and the tracker from abroad. Email is the follow-up and the paper trail; the phone is the closer. Call scripts carry a [CALLER] slot rather than a name, because either partner may take the call.
2.5 · The rules we work inside
CASL — Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation
Penalties up to $1M for an individual and $10M for a corporation, and the burden of proof sits on the sender. Consent here is implied via conspicuous publication: the business published the address itself, without a statement refusing unsolicited mail, and the message is relevant to their role.


No lead is emailed without a Source URL and a Date collected. If the address cannot be traced to a page the business published, the lead is call only and never emailed — 19 of the 37 are.


Every email carries: the sender's real name, a clear statement it is sent on behalf of Otis Commercial Cleaning, the mailing address 2447 Madison Ave, Montreal QC H4B 2T5, and a working unsubscribe valid 60 days and actioned within 10 business days.


Two working rules follow from this:


* Contact-data brokers are never a source. ZoomInfo, RocketReach, Lusha, Apollo, ContactOut and the rest sell addresses the business never published, so they give no basis at all. They rank first for almost any "company + contact" search, which makes this an easy mistake to make quietly.
* Relevance counts as much as publication. An admissions@ address at a school is published, but it is not the facilities buyer — so that is a call, not an email.
Competition Act s.74.011 — sender identity
Never invent a sender name. False or misleading sender information is reviewable conduct, and s.74.011(3.2) removed the defence that nobody was actually deceived. Mail goes from info@otiscc.ca under a real person's name.
CNESST and the décret
* The attestation de conformité releases a client from liability for a contractor's unpaid contributions. Facilities managers ask for it.
* The décret carries a 0.50% payroll levy plus a 0.50% employee deduction, monthly reports by the 15th, and three-year record retention.
* The joint-liability rule makes the client liable for an underpriced rival's unpaid wages. That is not a compliance burden — it is the strongest argument in the whole pitch against a cheaper competitor, and it opens on the prospect's own exposure rather than on Otis.

PART FOUR — WHERE IT GOES
4.1 · Every completed job opens a contract
This is the step the whole plan exists to produce, and the one most likely to be skipped.


* The conversation is with somebody who has already watched the standard happen in their own building — not somebody being asked to take a claim on trust.
* It is written into the outreach as a branch, not left to whoever remembers. A job delivered and never followed by that conversation is a job done, not a client won.
* The tracker records contract stage and contract value per month, so the number is visible rather than hoped for.
* Recurring work is retention and expansion. It is where this leads, not where it starts.
4.2 · Nine reviews toward twenty
Reviews are the constraint on referrals, on being found, and on every close that follows.


The hard part is already done: every review on the profile is five stars. The gap is volume, not quality. The target is around 20, which is where the janitorial peer set sits — not hundreds, which is a house-cleaning number.
How to get there safely
Google's policy prohibits reviews based on a conflict of interest, naming "current or former employment… or other professional or personal affiliations", and prohibits incentives offered in exchange for a review.


* ❌ Not staff, not friends, not family. Those are conflict-of-interest reviews under the policy. They get filtered or removed, and at nine reviews a removal wave is visible. The profile itself can be restricted.
* ❌ Never attach the referral discount to a review. The discount in 3.3 is for referrals only. Tying it to a review makes it an incentivised review.
* ✅ Every past and present client, systematically. Every site, every one-off job, every building manager Otis has actually worked for. Real experiences, every one of them allowed — and that list (3.3, item 15) is where 20 comes from.
* ✅ Ask at completion, every time, as a habit. The walkthrough sign-off is the natural moment, and it costs nothing to build in now.
* ✅ Steer the wording toward commercial — janitorial, contract, our building, our office — without scripting it.
Alongside it, and equally free
* Fill the Services section on the Google Business Profile — an empty field today
* Turn on GBP messaging; resume local posts
* Point the Yelp listing at the right audience — it is filed under Carpet Cleaning, which is why it sent 2 visitors all year
* Clean the analytics — filter bots and exclude /web/login, so the baseline in 4.4 measures people rather than machines
4.3 · Revenue worth talking about
None of these is in the current pipeline. They are raised here so the choice is yours rather than left unsaid.


Event venues and agencies. Repeat, scheduled work with no cleaner to displace. The closest to what is already running, and the first worth opening. It is also the one sector quota currently unmet, so the next sourcing run leads with it.


Contractor relationships. Post-construction as a standing arrangement rather than job by job. One contractor produces recurring one-off work indefinitely. Blocked only on the décret question in 3.4.


Specialist add-ons. Window cleaning, carpet extraction, pressure washing, waste management. Expansion revenue from clients already won, which needs no acquisition at all.


Residential — open, and not yet discussed. Nothing about it has been decided. Your existing reviews already describe residential work, so the demand is there without any marketing at all. It is kept out of the commercial pipeline for now only for sequencing — the buyer, the price point, the crew rhythm and the marketing are all different, and it competes for the same capacity ceiling. Worth a proper conversation about whether you want it, and when.
